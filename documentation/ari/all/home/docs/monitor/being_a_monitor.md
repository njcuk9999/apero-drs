# Being an APERO Monitor

Want hands-on experience with real telescope data, a flexible
remote role that fits around your studies, and something
genuinely useful to put on your CV? Become an **APERO Monitor**.

## What is an APERO Monitor?

APERO Monitors are the people who help keep the instrument data
reduction pipelines running smoothly day-to-day. You don't need
to be a pipeline developer — you need curiosity, attention to
detail, and a willingness to learn.

As a monitor you might:

- Keep an eye on the nightly data reduction "health" of the
  instruments — spotting when something looks unusual.
- Help triage and follow up on flagged issues or failed checks.
- Support observers and instrument teams with quick questions
  about data quality.
- Help review and verify target/object information.
- Suggest improvements to the documentation as you learn the
  ropes (the best documentation is often written by the newest
  monitor!).

## Why become a monitor?

- **Learn by doing.** There's no better way to understand how an
  astronomical pipeline actually works than seeing real data move
  through it.
- **Build real skills.** Data quality control, troubleshooting,
  communication with a science team — all transferable skills for
  research, industry, or further study.
- **Flexible & remote.** Fit it around your existing schedule —
  most tasks can be done whenever suits you.
- **Be part of a team.** You'll work alongside experienced
  instrument scientists and other monitors, with support always
  available.
- **It looks great on a CV/cover letter** — instrumentation,
  pipelines, and "operational" experience are highly valued.

## What experience do I need?

None! We're looking for people who are:

- Reliable and happy to check in regularly (even just a little
  bit of time each week helps).
- Comfortable learning new tools (some basic Python / command
  line familiarity helps, but is not required to get started).
- Curious about how astronomical instruments and pipelines work.

Training and documentation are provided, and there's always
someone you can ask.

## Instrument access

Some instruments require additional agreements or consortium
membership before monitor access can be granted — so applying
doesn't guarantee immediate access, but please apply anyway and
we'll help you figure out the next steps.

### SPIRou

SPIRou monitor access requires membership of at least one of the
large SPIRou surveys (e.g. SLS, PLANETS, amakihi, amongst others).
Potential monitors should still apply, but special access may need
to be discussed before monitor status can be granted.

### NIRPS

NIRPS monitor access requires membership of the NIRPS consortium
science team. Please email
[Lison Malo](mailto:lison.malo@umontreal.ca) (lison.malo@umontreal.ca)
to confirm institutional access and find out who your local
representative is.

## How do I get started?

Fill in the short
["Become a Monitor"](/monitor_portal/become_monitor) form. Tell us a little
about yourself — your career stage, institution, and which
instrument(s) you're interested in — and a member of the team
will get in touch to help you get set up.

Once you're a monitor, head to the
[Monitor Portal](/monitor_portal) for the day-to-day tools, and
the [Monitoring documentation](/docs/monitor) and
[How-to guides](/docs/monitor/how_to) for step-by-step
instructions.
